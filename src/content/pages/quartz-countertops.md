## Quartz Countertops for Utah Kitchens and Baths

Quartz is the material we install more than any other, and for good reason. It is engineered from natural quartz bound with resin, which makes it non-porous, consistent from slab to slab, and almost maintenance-free. It never needs sealing, it shrugs off coffee and wine, and it comes in everything from quiet solids to bold marble-look veining. For a busy Utah household, it is the surface that looks the same in ten years as it did on installation day.

Graebridge Stone fabricates quartz in our own shop in Lindon and installs it across Utah County, the Salt Lake Valley, and the Park City and Heber area. We carry Cambria, Caesarstone, Cosentino (Silestone), Della Terra, MSI, Pental, and Stratus, and we can source other lines on request.

### Why homeowners choose quartz

- **Non-porous.** Nothing soaks in, so there is no sealing and no staining from everyday spills.
- **Consistent.** What you pick in the shop is what arrives at your house. Veined patterns can be book-matched across seams and waterfall edges.
- **Scratch and chip resistant.** Quartz is one of the hardest surfaces available for a kitchen. Cutting boards are still a good idea; so are trivets, since quartz is heat resistant but not heat proof.
- **Easy to live with.** Soap, water, and a soft cloth. That is the whole care routine.

### Popular quartz looks right now

Calacatta and Carrara-inspired whites with soft grey or gold veining lead every showroom conversation. Warm neutrals (taupe, greige, cream) are close behind in Utah County remodels. Concrete-look and matte-finish quartz work well in modern homes, and leathered or suede finishes hide fingerprints on dark colors.

### Quartz vs. granite vs. quartzite

Choose quartz if you want low maintenance and a consistent pattern. Choose [granite](/granite-countertops/) if you want a natural, one-of-a-kind slab and do not mind sealing it now and then. Choose [quartzite](/quartzite-marble-countertops/) if you want a true natural stone with marble's look and more hardness than marble offers. We are happy to put samples of all three side by side.

### Frequently asked questions about quartz

**Does quartz need to be sealed?** No. Quartz is non-porous and never needs sealing.

**Can I put a hot pan on quartz?** Use a trivet. Quartz tolerates warm dishes, but direct high heat can discolor the resin.

**How thick are quartz countertops?** Most kitchen installations use 3 cm slabs; 2 cm is available for some applications and mitered edges can create a thicker look.

**Can quartz be used outdoors?** Standard quartz is not rated for direct sun. For outdoor kitchens we recommend [porcelain or granite](/#specialty).
