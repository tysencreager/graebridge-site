### Quartzite: marble's look, granite's toughness

Quartzite is a natural metamorphic stone that often reads as white or soft grey with flowing veins, which is why it is so often mistaken for marble. The difference is hardness: true quartzite is harder than granite, resists scratches and heat, and does not etch from lemon juice or vinegar the way marble does. It is the stone we recommend most often to people who love the marble look but cook every night. Like granite, it needs a penetrating sealer every year or two.

### Marble: the classic, with honest expectations

Marble is beautiful, cool to the touch, and the traditional surface for baths, bars, and baking stations. It is also softer and more porous than quartzite or granite, so it will etch from acids and can scratch. Many clients embrace that patina; others prefer marble in a powder room, a vanity, or a wet bar where it sees gentler use. A honed finish hides etching better than polished. We will tell you plainly where marble will and will not make you happy.

### Soapstone: warm, forgiving, and heat-proof

Soapstone is dense, non-porous, and nearly impervious to heat; a hot pan straight from the oven is fine. It starts a soft grey and deepens toward charcoal over time, especially with occasional mineral oil, and small scratches sand out by hand. It suits farmhouse, cottage, and transitional kitchens, and it is one of the few natural stones that never needs sealing.

### Which natural stone is right for you?

- **You cook daily and want the marble look:** quartzite.
- **A bath, bar, or lightly used surface where elegance comes first:** marble.
- **A warm, matte, lived-in kitchen that can take heat:** soapstone.
- **Natural stone with maximum durability and the widest color range:** [granite](/granite-countertops/).
- **No sealing, ever:** [quartz](/quartz-countertops/) or [porcelain](/#porcelain).

### Frequently asked questions

**Is quartzite the same as quartz?** No. Quartzite is a natural stone; quartz countertops are engineered from ground quartz and resin. Quartzite needs sealing; quartz does not.

**Will marble stain?** It can if spills sit. Sealing helps, prompt wiping helps more, and a honed finish makes etching far less visible.

**Does soapstone need sealing?** No. Mineral oil is optional and only changes the color.
