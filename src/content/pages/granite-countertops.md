## Granite Countertops, Cut and Installed in Utah

Granite is the original luxury countertop, and it is still the right answer for a lot of Utah kitchens. Every slab is quarried stone, so no two are alike; the movement, flecks, and color shifts in your countertop exist nowhere else. Granite handles heat better than any engineered surface, resists scratches, and, with occasional sealing, resists stains for decades.

Graebridge Stone has fabricated granite for more than thirty years. We template, cut, edge, and polish in our Lindon shop and install throughout Utah County, the Salt Lake Valley, and the Park City and Heber area.

### Why granite still wins

- **Heat resistant.** A hot pan straight from the oven will not mark it.
- **One of a kind.** You choose the exact slab, and we lay out the cuts so the best movement lands on your island.
- **Hard-wearing.** Granite resists scratching and chipping in daily use and can be refinished if it ever needs it.
- **Value.** Natural granite remains one of the surfaces buyers recognize and pay for in a Utah home.

### Caring for granite

Granite is a natural stone, so it is slightly porous. A penetrating sealer applied every year or two keeps oil and wine from soaking in; it takes about twenty minutes and we show you how. Day to day, soap and water is all it needs. Avoid harsh acidic cleaners, which can dull the polish over time.

### Colors and finishes

Utah homeowners are choosing lighter granites (whites, creams, and soft greys with subtle veining) alongside the classic blacks and browns. Polished is the standard finish; honed and leathered finishes give a softer, matte look that hides fingerprints and suits modern and farmhouse kitchens. Edge profiles include eased, bevel, bullnose, ogee, and mitered waterfall.

### Granite vs. quartz

Choose granite for natural character, heat tolerance, and a surface that can be refinished. Choose [quartz](/quartz-countertops/) if you want zero sealing and a repeatable pattern. If you love marble's look but worry about etching, look at [quartzite](/quartzite-marble-countertops/), a natural stone with granite-like hardness.

### Frequently asked questions about granite

**How often should granite be sealed?** Every one to two years for most slabs; lighter and more porous stones a little more often. If water no longer beads on the surface, it is time.

**Is granite more expensive than quartz?** It overlaps. Entry-level granite often costs less than quartz; exotic slabs cost more. We will show you options at several price points.

**Can granite go outdoors?** Yes. Granite is one of the best choices for outdoor kitchens and BBQ surrounds in Utah's climate.
